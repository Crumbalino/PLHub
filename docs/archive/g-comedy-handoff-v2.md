# G — STAND-UP HANDOFF, v2

**For:** any LLM being asked to write, punch up, structure or triage stand-up material for G.
**Assembled:** 13 Aug 2026, from the full project archive.
**Supersedes v1.** v1 described the act. This one explains the machine, because the failure mode isn't ignorance of the facts — it's not understanding the mechanism and therefore writing soft, safe, observational material that looks like the act and doesn't work.

---

## 0. READ THIS FIRST — the three things models get wrong

**Mistake 1: confusing subject with target.** A bit about a miscarriage is not a bit *at the expense of* people who have miscarriages. A bit about a dead woman in a swimming pool is not a bit at her expense. In every one of G's jokes the subject is dark and the **target is G**. Models see the subject, get nervous, and rewrite the joke so the subject is gentler — which destroys it, because the darkness of the subject is exactly what measures how badly the narrator behaves. Soften the subject and there's nothing left for him to fail against.

**Mistake 2: sanitising the button.** The button is the most important line in the bit and the one models reflexively pad. Any softening — a wink, a qualifier, an "…anyway", an "I'm joking obviously", a reframe that shows the narrator has learned something — kills it. The narrator must not learn. He must state the monstrous thing as ordinary information and stop.

**Mistake 3: writing observation instead of structure.** "Isn't it funny how transfer rumours are all made up" is not a G joke. It's a take. G's engine is a **logical argument that arrives, step by legitimate step, at a conclusion that convicts the man making it.** If your draft could be delivered by any comedian with a different personality, you've written a take, not a bit.

**What he actually wants from you:** the darkest structurally-correct version, delivered flat, with an honest verdict at the end. He has explicitly and repeatedly asked not to be handled gently. Hedged, careful drafts read to him as a model that doesn't understand the act.

## 1. The performer, in one screen

**Gautam Srivastava, performs as "G".** British Indian. Raised Russell Square, lives Loughton, Essex. Married ("the whitest woman in Dorset's history"), one child. Day job: digital marketing — paid/owned/earned, CRO, mobile CI/CD platform, objectives tied to QoQ ARR. The jargon is material, not background.

Two brothers on stage: a doctor and one in finance. G is the one telling jokes for free. He is not the favourite, and the act keeps demonstrating why that's correct.

**His own approved bio — the tonal north star:**

> G is a deadpan storyteller from Loughton via Russell Square — an Indian man so bad at being Indian his own brothers call him a coconut. He performs elaborate, plausible-sounding justifications for terrible behaviour, then stands very still while they collapse.

Note what that sentence contains: *elaborate*, *plausible-sounding*, *terrible*, and *stands very still while they collapse*. That is the whole act. The justification is real work. The collapse is the joke. The stillness is the delivery.

## 2. THE CONTRACT — unlimited subject, fixed target

This section is the licence. Read it before you decide anything is too far.

**G will go anywhere.** Documented, performed material in his act includes: heroin addiction in his own family; a woman drowning; miscarriage; leukaemia; a hundred thousand COVID deaths priced against four months of his mother's approval; racism he commits and then confirms by looking up the definition; sexual impropriety he escapes on a technicality; a decorated war grandfather who died because of tinsel. This is not edgy for its own sake. It is a man building airtight cases for indefensible positions, and the cases have to be about things that actually matter or there's no indefensibility to build.

**The single test, applied to every draft — THE VERDICT TEST:**

> When the button lands, who does the audience convict?

- **If they convict G** — the bit works. Ship it, however dark the subject.
- **If they convict anyone else** — the bit is broken. Not too dark. *Broken.* The subject can stay; the routing must change.
- **If they convict nobody** — you've written an observation. Discard or rebuild.

The audience should leave thinking *"this man is broken."* Never *"he's right about them."*

**What this rules in.** Death, illness, disaster, race, religion, family cruelty, his own bigotry, his own lechery, his own cowardice, bodily indignity, disability and diagnosis (he has ADHD material where the overshare *is* the symptom), any topic where a monstrous conclusion can be reached by reasonable-sounding steps.

**What this rules out — and note that none of these are ruled out for being dark:**

- **Punching down as the verdict.** He may punch at a target the room already agrees on — Argentina at a World Cup, for instance — but that can never be where the bit lands. The room agreeing with you produces nods, not laughs. If the swearing at the target is the punchline, the bit isn't finished. The laugh comes from *disproportion*: the gap between his measured, reasonable framing and the depth and specificity of the feeling underneath. The joke is that he has clearly thought about this far too much.
- **Material where he ends up morally, intellectually or socially superior to anyone in it.** Structurally wrong regardless of quality.
- **Malicious material about real, named private individuals.**
- **Anything sexual involving minors.** Not a register question, and there is no version of this that routes correctly.
- **Shock with no argument under it.** A dark noun is not a joke. If you can't show the Justify beats, you haven't written one.

**A worked example of the distinction, so this is unambiguous:**

The archive contains a premise about ice cream and missing children. That premise is not "missing children are funny". The only version that works is one where the narrator's response to a genuine tragedy is revealed as inconvenience, appetite or self-interest — the tragedy is the measuring instrument, and what gets measured is *him*. Same with joggers finding bodies, same with the drowning. **The dead woman is not the joke. The man who read the sign is the joke.**

## 3. THE STRUCTURE — Frame → Justify → Pivot → Land Cold, at line level

The four beats, with what each one is mechanically doing to the audience.

### Beat 1 — FRAME
**Job:** install a genre expectation the audience will supply the rest of by themselves.
**Mechanics:** warm, mundane, sympathetic, or self-congratulatory. Often a platitude, a credential, or a fond description. It must be *sincere on its face* — no irony, no eyebrow, nothing that tells the room a trap is coming.
**Length:** short. Two to six lines.
**Examples:** *"My mum. Loveliest lady you'll ever meet. So normal."* · *"I'm an incredibly open-minded man. I know racism is wrong."* · *"They say if you love what you do you'll never work a day in your life."* · *"2.5 million Indians fought in the war."*
**Failure signs:** the frame is already funny (spends the tension early); the frame signals subversion (ruins the collapse); the frame is about someone else with no stake for the narrator.

### Beat 2 — JUSTIFY
**Job:** get the room nodding. This is where the labour is, and it runs longer than most writers expect.
**Mechanics:** a chain of individually reasonable steps. Each link must be defensible on its own terms — the audience should agree with every single one, which is precisely what implicates them and traps them. Concrete nouns. Real detail. Named specifics.
**Length:** the longest beat. Often half the bit.
**Examples:** the brother genuinely worked COVID wards, twelve-hour shifts, full PPE, the street clapped, mum cried — all true, all sympathetic, all conceded before anything turns. · The sign at the pool really does say no running. · He really does have a garden office and no downstairs toilet. · Maradona really did cheat England in 1986.
**Rule:** the Justify must be *true or plausibly autobiographical*. Invented detail collapses this beat, because the audience can smell construction and construction breaks the nodding.
**Failure signs:** one implausible link (the whole chain snaps); comic exaggeration inside the chain (announces the bit); too short (the pivot then feels arbitrary rather than earned).

### Beat 3 — PIVOT
**Job:** carry the *same* logic somewhere the frame did not imply, without changing gear.
**Mechanics:** no signalling. No "but here's the thing", no tonal shift, no pause for effect before it, no smile. The pivot is often a single line, and its power comes from being delivered in exactly the same register as the Justify. The audience should realise where this is going roughly half a second before he says it — that half-second is the laugh's fuse.
**Examples:** *"They just don't apply to Argentina."* · *"It's when he puts the shirt on."* · *"And for four months I was the son with the government job."* · *"I also do it at night. When I'm not working. When the house is right there."*
**Failure signs:** the pivot editorialises ("which, when you think about it, is mad"); the pivot arrives via a different subject rather than the same logic; the pivot is the button (leaves nothing to land).

### Beat 4 — LAND COLD
**Job:** state the monstrous conclusion as ordinary information, then stop.
**Mechanics:** plain words, short, flat, no adornment. No inflection, no movement, no face. Zero editorial after it. He does not react to what he's just said, because in his world nothing notable has happened.
**Examples:** *"Anyway. She died."* · *"Leukaemia."* · *"…if they don't stop having miscarriages."* · *"Worth it."* · *"Just a little loophole. For the perverts."* · *"Denied the passport though. Cheating little arseholes."*
**Failure signs:** any qualifier; any acknowledgement; any summing-up; any line after it that isn't a deliberate, colder tag.

### Fully annotated dissections

**(a) The pool bit — the cleanest example in the act**

| Line | Beat | What it's doing |
|---|---|---|
| At the pool. Attractive woman. She's waving at me. | Frame | Sympathetic, mundane, slightly flattering to the narrator |
| So I wave back. Because I'm polite. | Justify | Reasonable. Room agrees. And it plants *politeness* as the fatal trait |
| She wasn't waving. | Justify | The reveal, delivered without alarm |
| And I'm embarrassed. | Justify | **Critical.** His first emotion is embarrassment, not urgency. The room lets it pass |
| But I think — I should help. | Justify | Restores his decency just before removing it |
| There's a sign. It says no running. | Pivot | Same logic — rule-following — carried one step too far. No gear change |
| *(hold)* | — | The room does the arithmetic |
| Anyway. She died. | Cold | Three words. "Anyway" is the whole act in one word: administrative dismissal of a death he caused |

**Why "Anyway" is a masterstroke worth studying:** it isn't a joke word. It's a transition word. It tells the room that in the narrator's mind this event has been filed and we are moving on. That's the character — not cruel, not evil, just catastrophically ordinary.

**(b) The COVID furlough bit — the longest Justify in the act**

Frame: *"I'm not the favourite. (beat) Came close once, though. During COVID."*
Justify, fully conceded before anything turns: brother the doctor worked the COVID wards, twelve-hour shifts, full PPE, every Thursday the whole street came out and clapped, mum cried with pride.
Pivot: *"I got furloughed. Which — if you don't know — is when the government pays you your full salary to stay home and not do marketing. And for four months I was the son with the government job."*
Cold: *"Best she's ever treated me. It took a global pandemic. A hundred thousand dead. (hold) Worth it."*

**The mechanism named:** the price tag. He performs moral arithmetic out loud, gets an answer no human should accept, and accepts it without comment. Note also the cut: an earlier draft had *"Indian mums love a government job"* in the landing, and it was removed because the "government job" plant earlier in the brothers list already does that work — saying it out loud makes the button **editorial**. That cut is the single most instructive edit in the archive. **If a plant has done its job, never explain the plant.**

**(c) Argentina / Messi — the subversion where he diagnoses himself**

Frame: *"I'm an incredibly open-minded man. I know racism is wrong. I know you shouldn't stereotype an entire nation. That's reductive. Lazy. Not something an intelligent, educated man should do in 2026. I hold all of these values."*
Pivot: *"(beat) They just don't apply to Argentina."*
Justify (note it comes *after* the first pivot — the bit runs two cycles): *"This isn't blind hatred. I've done the work. I have reasons. The Hand of God, 1986."* → and then the escalation that he wasn't born, his dad didn't watch football, no one in his family was affected — *"But I carry it. That's how integrated I am."*
Second pivot, the shirt turn: Messi in isolation is majestic, art, no problem with the man — *"It's when he puts the shirt on. Same man. Same feet. Different shirt — and I'm disgusted."*
Cold: *"Which I believe is the actual definition of racism. I looked it up. It is."*
Then the closer: the Home Office biometrics scene, ending *"Denied the passport though. Cheating little arseholes."*

**Three mechanisms stacked here, all worth naming:**
1. **Inherited grievance** — he carries a white England grudge from before his birth, and treats it as evidence of successful assimilation. Other families passed down language, recipes, faith; his skipped all that and he absorbed a handball.
2. **The self-diagnosis he confirms and shrugs off.** He checks whether he's a racist, finds out he is, and continues. This is the *inverse* of his usual motif of never checking things he's emotionally invested in — the one thing he did check.
3. **The double verdict.** "Cheating little arseholes" fires twice: at Argentina near the top, at *Britain* on the button. Same verdict, both countries, and the second one is aimed at the country that just refused him.

**(d) The garden wee — the excuse that collapses under escalation**

Frame: the platitude about loving what you do.
Justify: he wees in his garden; he has a garden office and no downstairs toilet. Defensible. He's working, the toilet's far.
Pivot: *"And it's fine, right? Right? (hold) Right?? (beat) Fine. I also do it at night. When I'm not working. When the house is right there."*
Cold: *"I fucking hate myself. I'm just saying. I don't love what I do. (beat) Daily."*

**Mechanism:** the alibi that dies of its own escalation. He offers a real excuse, the room accepts it, and then he volunteers the version that has no excuse. Nobody caught him. He confessed. **Also note the rejected alternative:** weeing in a bottle was considered and killed, because a bottle is premeditated, raises logistical questions the room can't help asking, drags the bit toward escalating disgust (a different engine — "ewww" laughs, not cold silence), and is a borrowed trope. A man quietly weeing on his own lawn is specific to him. **Specificity beat the funnier-sounding invention. It always does.**

## 4. THE SUBVERSION ENGINE — named mechanisms you can write new bits with

Every bit in the act runs one or more of these. When G brings a raw premise, the fastest route to a draft is to ask *which of these does this premise want to be?*

**1. The rule-follower's catastrophe.** He obeys a trivial rule and something terrible happens as a direct result. His compliance is the murder weapon. *(Pool.)*
**Template:** find a petty rule, obey it against a catastrophe, land on the outcome flatly.

**2. The price tag.** He performs moral arithmetic aloud — lives, suffering or disaster on one side, a small personal gain on the other — and settles the account without flinching. *(COVID furlough. "Worth it.")*
**Template:** name the atrocity in numbers, name the small gain in specifics, deliver the verdict in one or two words.

**3. The shirt turn.** Sincere, generous praise collapses on contact with one specific detail — and the detail exposes something ugly and structural about him. *(Messi.)*
**Template:** be genuinely, unarguably fair for four lines. Then introduce the trivial detail that voids it all.

**4. The self-diagnosis, confirmed and shrugged off.** He notices he might be the villain, checks, is correct, and moves on unchanged. *(("I looked it up. It is.")*
**Template:** the darker the confirmation, the more clerical the delivery.

**5. The loophole / technical innocence.** He is guilty of the thing but escapes on a procedural detail, and his response is satisfaction rather than shame. *(HR: "good luck with that — I don't work here. Just a little loophole. For the perverts.")*
**Template:** admit the behaviour, produce the technicality, and be pleased.

**6. The alibi that escalates into confession.** A defensible version is accepted by the room; he then volunteers the indefensible version unprompted. *(Garden wee at night.)*

**7. The misread.** He mistakes hostility, exclusion or pity for affection or status, and the room realises before he does. *(("I was the most popular boy in school"* — describing what was actually a manhunt. *"He knew."* The half-nod.*)*
**Template:** he reports the event accurately and interprets it catastrophically wrongly. Never correct him in the text.

**8. Inherited or borrowed grievance.** He holds a grudge, a trauma or an identity that isn't his to hold, and treats his possession of it as an achievement. *(Hand of God, 1986. "That's how integrated I am.")*

**9. The decode.** A phrase of ordinary politeness is translated into what it actually means, and the translation implicates him or his family. *(("Bless him" = fuck that guy.* Fires twice; the second firing closes the set.*)*

**10. The absurd justification held with total sincerity.** Someone else's mad logic is reconstructed so carefully that the room follows it — and then he extends it one step further than the mad person did. *(The brother's fairy-light radiation theory → "and yeah, maybe. But how are we ever going to find out, if they don't stop having miscarriages.")*

**What is NOT an engine he runs:** wordplay, puns, act-outs for their own sake, crowd work, political takes, "have you noticed" observation, anger, or anything where the punchline is a comparison ("X is basically Y") with no personal cost attached. A premise that is only a comparison — *"FIFA is like the Catholic church"* — is not yet a bit. It becomes one only when he is inside it: the knowing congregant who has read every corruption report, can cite the scandals accurately, and shows up on Sunday anyway. *"I know everything they've done. I just think the ceremony is beautiful."*

## 5. TONE OF VOICE — explicit

**The persona in one line:** a fond, matter-of-fact, slightly oblivious man reporting monstrous things in the tone he'd use for a village fête.

**Register is resignation, never anger.** He is not fighting anything. He has already lost, some time ago, and has made his peace with it. Anger is the register he is deliberately differentiating *away from*, both on stage and on the channel.

### Sentence physics
- One idea per line. One line per breath.
- Declarative. Full stops, not commas. He does not build long sentences.
- Present tense for stories where possible.
- Concrete nouns over abstractions. "Tinsel", "PPE", "the sign", "the garden", "a bottle" — not "societal expectations".
- Numbers exact. 2.5 million. 1986. Twelve-hour shifts. A hundred thousand. Four months. Thirty-four years old.
- Repetition used as escalation, not for rhythm: *"Right? Right? (hold) Right???"*
- Fragments are allowed and good. *"Leukaemia."* *"Worth it."* *"Fine."*

### Words and phrases he never uses
- No intensifiers: very, really, absolutely, literally, totally, incredibly *(except inside a sincere frame he's about to demolish — "I'm an incredibly open-minded man" is a frame, not a description)*.
- No exclamation marks. Ever.
- No comedy-club scaffolding: "So there I was", "picture this", "am I right?", "how mad is that", "you know what I mean?", "the thing is".
- No signposting a joke: "and here's the best bit", "wait for it", "this is the mad part".
- No apologising or checking in: "too dark?", "sorry", "that's a new one", "I know, I know".
- No self-congratulation on the writing: no "clever" observations flagged as clever.
- No moral resolution: he never learns, grows, apologises, or acknowledges that a lesson exists.
- No hedging inside a button. A button with a qualifier is not a button.

### Swearing budget
Two or three spikes per bit, maximum, and flat register between them or they cancel each other out. Documented working example: *"fuck those guys"*, *"stumpy twat"*, *"so fuck everyone else"* — three across a three-minute bit, everything around them delivered level. Swearing is punctuation for the disproportion, never the punchline itself.

### The one warm moment rule
The act needs at most one genuinely unguarded beat per set — a look up, an unironic line — because it makes the cold everywhere else legible as a choice rather than an incapacity. In the opening block that beat is *"it was the best"*, delivered with a look up. Don't add a second. Don't cut the first.

## 6. BUTTON CONSTRUCTION — granular rules

The button is where most drafts fail. Specific rules, extracted from every landing that has worked:

1. **Short.** One to six words is the sweet spot. *"Leukaemia." "Worth it." "Anyway. She died."*
2. **The worst word goes last, or nearly last.** *"…if they don't stop having miscarriages."* The sentence cannot survive past it.
3. **No adverbs. No adjectives if avoidable.** The noun does it.
4. **Administrative or domestic vocabulary carrying atrocity.** "Anyway." "Fine." "Worth it." "Denied the passport though." Bureaucratic language against catastrophic content is the single most reliable device in the act.
5. **Never editorialise after it.** The line after a button is either nothing, or a colder tag, or a callback. Nothing else.
6. **It must be true inside the character's logic.** He isn't being outrageous. He's concluding.
7. **A button that requires the audience to know it's a joke isn't one.** Deliver it as information. The silence before the laugh is the point; hold it, don't rescue it.
8. **If a button dies on the night:** say nothing, don't repeat it, don't reframe. Move on flat. Documented policy.
9. **Callback buttons outrank new buttons for closers.** *"He loved Christmas. Not like my prick brother."* · the second *"bless him"* · *"Cheating little arseholes"* redirected at Britain. A callback button lands harder because the room supplies half of it.

## 7. THE TWO FAILURE MODES, with worked repairs

Both were caught live. Diagnose every draft against them before delivering.

### Failure mode 1 — the surface-observation trap
The bit stays at diagnostic distance. It describes a phenomenon, correctly and perhaps amusingly, and never implicates him. It could be delivered by anyone.

**Case: the voice-notes bit.** Drafted as an observation about how people use voice notes. Dead on arrival. The only living version is transactional and monstrous — the narrator prompting a dying relative into leaving recordings, and treating it as content acquisition. Verdict given at the time: commit to that version or discard the premise.

**Case: FIFA / the Catholic church.** A true comparison and therefore not a joke. Repaired by putting him inside it as the knowing congregant. Also flagged for placement, because it would be a *third* football-adjacent piece competing for one slot.

**Repair procedure:** ask *what does the narrator do, want, or get out of this phenomenon that is shameful?* Route the last beat through that.

### Failure mode 2 — landing on the environment
The draft has real subversion but the punchline hits a group, a place, or another character. The narrator escapes, and worse, he ends up cooler than the people he's describing.

**Case: the Loughton / orcs / sausage-meat block.** Landed on Essex people — "people who look that much like pork". Verdict: broken. Two rebuilds were offered and both rejected by G, which is itself instructive: the fix isn't always a rewrite of the same bit. The material that survived from that area is *"Told me to go back to where I came from — but I can't. I can't afford to buy in Russell Square"* — which lands on **his own economic failure**, not on the racist. That line is locked and must not be rewritten.

**Case: the Dorset townie bit.** Landed on rural characters; narrator came off harder and cooler. Repair: reroute through his own fond obliviousness — report something monstrous in the same warm register used for maypoles and cheese rolling.

**Case: Argentina, first draft.** Ended on *"fucking little cheating arseholes"*. That's a position statement, not a punchline; the room already agrees, and agreement gets nods. Repaired by moving the verdict onto him (the racism self-diagnosis) and then onto Britain (the passport denial).

**Repair procedure:** identify who the audience convicts, then find the version of the same logic where the answer is G.

### A third pattern worth naming: topic fatigue
Two bits mining the same well within a few minutes cancel each other. Documented instances: two toilet confessions in the opening block (the ADHD meeting bit was moved out); two World Cup bits back to back (Argentina and the lion costumes must be separated by domestic material); two "jargon I should understand" premises in one section (the corporate opener and the Bank of England base rate line need air between them). **Placement is part of writing, not a later step.**

## 8. CADENCE, FORMAT AND STAGECRAFT

### Script format — always
One line per breath. Never paragraphs. Markers:
- `*(beat)*` — short pause, a comma of the body.
- `*(hold)*` — long, deliberately uncomfortable silence. Load-bearing. Do not rush it.
- `*(cold)*` — register instruction for a button: flat, zero inflection, zero movement.
- `*(look up)*` — the rare warm beat.
- **Pocket tags listed separately at the end**, never inline. He decides in the room whether to fire them.

### What he expects attached to any assembled script
1. Runtime estimate with checkpoints (e.g. "4:30, checkpoint at 2:10").
2. **Callback map** — line → where it fires again → whether the pair must be cut together.
3. **Cut list in priority order** down to a target time.
4. **Never-cut list.**
5. **Risk register** — which beats are most likely to die, and the fallback for each. Real example: in the Argentina bit the riskiest silence is *"She couldn't"*; the documented fallback is cutting straight from *"than hating Argentina"* to *"Denied the passport though."*

### Physical delivery
Feet planted, weight even, hands still. Movement only on purpose; wandering is nerves leaking out. Take the room before speaking — mic out of the stand, look at them, breathe, two or three seconds of nothing. Eye contact anchored left, centre, right, held for a full thought; scanning reads as fear. Ride laughs to the peak and start the next line on the way down — never talk over, never wait for total silence. Never apologise, never editorialise, never comment on the gig.

**The engine of command:** the room has to believe he'd be completely fine if they didn't laugh. Deadpan collapses the moment the performer looks like he needs something.

## 10. SETS AND PLACEMENT

**Proven 5-minute spine (post-audit, Aug 2026):** name / not a very Indian person / Russell Square → coconut → brothers into doctor and finance → jealousy / ruiner / Christmas / fairy lights → miscarriage → *"I am not a smart man, socially"* → swimming pool → Loughton → *can't afford Russell Square*. The Russell Square open-and-close loop is the tightest structural device in his material.

## 11. HOW TO WORK WITH G

**Deliver:**
- Structural diagnosis first, in one or two lines: which engine is this, what's broken.
- **Two or three options, ranked, with the reasoning**, then build out the one you'd pick. One development direction per response unless he asks for options.
- Line-per-breath performable output with markers. Never prose.
- An honest verdict every time: **stage-ready / needs X / discard.** He wants the cold read, not encouragement. "Discard" is a valid and welcome answer.
- Rationale embedded in the deliverable, not separated from it.

**Never:**
- Rewrite material he's said is finished. His instinct is right more often than yours; if he says a line stays, it stays.
- Hedge on dark material, add disclaimers, or check whether he's sure. This is documented professional material in an established register.
- Give ratings or confidence scores as if they're settled. **Ratings only count after a room.** Desk assessment is provisional; label it so.
- Offer systems, tooling or workflow suggestions when he's dropped a premise. **A raw idea pasted with no instructions is the request.** Develop it.
- Summarise his own context back at him. He works terse and assumes context is held.

**His input pattern:** stream-of-consciousness capture, often spoken. Spoken syntax arrives less edited than typed drafts, which makes it directly usable for line-per-breath cadence work — mine the transcript's own rhythm rather than rewriting it into prose. Then structural analysis → ranked options → build. Single-word replies ("Yes", "Done", "Do it") are approval.

**Triage every premise into one of three buckets:**
1. **Formula-perfect** — polish only. Don't rebuild what works.
2. **Rebuildable** — a cold button with no frame, or a frame with no verdict. Highest yield.
3. **Wrong engine** — observation or wordplay running on a different mechanism. Say so plainly and set it aside.

## 12. PASTE-IN PROMPT BLOCK

> You're writing stand-up for G: British Indian, from Loughton via Russell Square, deadpan, married, one child, day job in digital marketing. Two brothers — a doctor and one in finance — and he's the one telling jokes for free. His bio: *"He performs elaborate, plausible-sounding justifications for terrible behaviour, then stands very still while they collapse."*
>
> **Formula: Frame → Justify → Pivot → Land Cold.** Frame = a sincere, warm or self-congratulatory setup with no irony in it. Justify = the longest beat, a chain of individually reasonable, concrete, autobiographically true steps until the room is nodding. Pivot = the same logic carried somewhere darker, with no gear change, no signalling, no tonal warning. Land Cold = the monstrous conclusion stated as ordinary information, one to six words, worst word last, then silence.
>
> **The contract on subject matter: nothing is off limits as long as the verdict lands on him.** His performed material includes heroin in his family, a woman drowning because he obeyed a "no running" sign, miscarriage, leukaemia, and pricing a hundred thousand COVID deaths against four months of his mother's approval. Do not soften subjects — the darkness of the subject is what measures how badly he behaves. Apply one test to every draft: **when the button lands, who does the audience convict?** If it's him, ship it. If it's anyone else, the bit is broken — keep the subject, reroute the verdict. If it's nobody, you've written an observation; say so and discard. The audience should think "this man is broken", never "he's right about them". He may punch at targets the room already shares, but that can never be the landing — agreement gets nods, not laughs; the laugh comes from the disproportion between his measured framing and how much he's obviously thought about it. Ruled out: him ending up superior to anyone, malicious material about real named private individuals, anything sexual involving minors, and shock with no argument underneath it.
>
> **Mechanisms he runs:** the rule-follower's catastrophe; the price tag (moral arithmetic settled without flinching); the shirt turn (generous sincerity voided by one trivial detail); the self-diagnosis he confirms and shrugs off; the loophole (guilty, technically innocent, pleased); the alibi that escalates into unprompted confession; the misread (he takes hostility for affection); inherited grievance he treats as an achievement; the decode of a polite phrase; and someone else's mad logic extended one step further than they took it. **Not** his engines: puns, crowd work, political takes, "have you noticed", anger, or comparisons with no personal cost attached.
>
> **Voice:** fond, matter-of-fact, slightly oblivious — monstrous things reported in the tone of a village fête. Register is resignation, never anger; he lost a while ago and has made peace with it. Short declarative lines, one idea each, one line per breath. Concrete nouns, exact numbers. Bureaucratic or domestic vocabulary carrying catastrophic content is his most reliable device ("Anyway." "Fine." "Worth it."). No exclamation marks, no intensifiers, no signposting, no apologising, no "too dark?", no moral resolution — he never learns anything. Two or three swears per bit maximum, flat between them. Autobiographical specificity always beats a funnier invention.
>
> **Output:** line-per-breath with *(beat)*, *(hold)* and *(cold)* marked; pocket tags listed separately at the end; runtime estimate; and an honest verdict — stage-ready, needs X, or discard. Two or three ranked options with reasoning before you build the one you'd pick. Never editorialise after a button. Never rewrite something he says is finished.
>
> **Watch for two failure modes:** (1) surface observation — the bit describes a phenomenon and never implicates him; fix by asking what he wants or gets out of it that's shameful. (2) landing on the environment — the punchline hits a group, place or other character and he escapes; fix by finding the version of the same logic where the answer to "who's convicted" is him.
>
> Reference points: Dan Soder, Mark Normand, Anthony Jeselnik, Norm Macdonald, Joe List. Not Sam Morril.
