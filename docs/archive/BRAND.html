Companion to THE_FOOTBALL_HUB.md and PAGE_SPEC.md. This document resolves §10 (Design direction) and closes open items 2 and 3 of §17. Where it disagrees with §10, this wins — §10 is superseded on colour, type and the wordmark, and stands on everything else.

One rule governs this document. No colour, type size or tier enters the system without a measured contrast figure against the ground. Every number here was calculated, not judged by eye.

1 · The idea

A board, not a page. Football sites all look alike because they inherited the newspaper sports section — big photo, big headline, feed below. A departure board is a different object: typographic, dense, information first, photography incidental.

Which means hierarchy comes from type, not from decoration. No borders, boxes, badges or background colours doing a job that size and weight should be doing. If a page needs decoration to communicate hierarchy, the typography is not working.

Calm canvas, personality at the edges. Reading surfaces stay clear. Personality lives in the mark, the loading states, the empty states, the referee's one interesting fact and the sign-off. Nowhere else.

The consequence of choosing a cool palette

Ice and rose are cool accents on a cool near-black, which is the same temperature family as FotMob, Sky, OneFootball and FPL. So the palette will not be what separates you at a glance — the typography and the layout have to do that work instead. That raises the stakes on the board direction above and on Atkinson as the text face. Worth knowing, not worth reopening.

The ground is not pure black. #FFFFFF on #000000 is 21:1 — the theoretical maximum and the wrong target, because at that separation white bleeds at the edge of every letter. Halation is worst on small text and it hits exactly the readers this site is built for. 18.56:1 is four times the AA requirement with the glare gone.

This is how the no-grey rule actually works. The dim tiers are not the ink at reduced opacity — that gives you #A6AAAE, which is a grey by any definition. They are the dimmed ink pushed 35% toward the ice, so every text tier sits in the same hue family as the accent and reads as cooled ink rather than dead grey. It also measures higher. Derive any new tier the same way.

Ice marks one thing: that a story has moved. Outlet counts, movement figures, the RISING state. Nothing else on the page is ever ice, which is why the reader learns it in a day without being told. The moment ice means two things it means nothing.

Rose appears in the logo and nowhere else. That is what keeps it feeling earned, and it means its lower ratio is never load-bearing.

Banned

  • Any grey hex. Not one, anywhere, for any reason.
  • Grass green. #2E8B57 measures 4.75:1 — an actual fail for body text, which is why football sites use it as a background rather than a colour.
  • Green-to-cyan gradients. Fantasy Premier League is #00FF87 → #02EFFF. Since availability and card data come out of the FPL API, resemblance would read as affiliation.
  • Red breaking-news banners, betting-app aesthetics, neon sports tones, stadium photography.
  • Gradients on letterforms. See §3.

Why three flat dots and not a gradient

A gradient has no single contrast value, so it cannot be verified — and the moment one exists in the system, every future use needs a special exemption. Three flat dots read as the ramp, carry the same idea, and every value in the brand stays measurable. An ellipsis also happens to mean this is still developing, which is the product.

Rules

  • Clear space: the cap height of the wordmark on all four sides. Nothing enters it.
  • Minimum sizes: wordmark 110px wide; dots 4px each with 3px gaps; favicon 16px.
  • Dot order never changes. Ice, bridge, rose, left to right, always.
  • Never outline the wordmark, add a shadow, place it on a photograph, stretch it, recolour it, put a football in it, or set it in anything but Archivo 800.
  • On light backgrounds (print, partner decks) the wordmark goes to ground and the dots stay unchanged.

Atkinson is the choice that carries the accessibility promise. It was commissioned by the Braille Institute and engineered so that no two characters can be confused — the tail on the l, the slab on the 1, the open C. Geometric faces like Poppins make I and l identical and use a single-storey a; that is the wrong trade for a product whose whole premise is scanning. Atkinson is also warm, slightly odd, and used by nothing in football.

Category label in mono. Headline in Archivo at h3 weight. The bones in Atkinson at 16px. Outlets in dim mono. The count in ice, because the count is the movement. Four tiers, no boxes, no dividers — the hierarchy is entirely type and colour.

Hard limits

  • 11px minimum font size. Anywhere, no exceptions.
  • 44×44px minimum touch target.
  • 320px minimum viewport.
  • Status is always a word. Never colour alone — WCAG 1.4.1. A dot may reinforce it.
  • Tokens are plain TypeScript objects, never CSS custom properties. Undefined CSS variables render components invisibly and pass every build check — that is what broke the site twice.
  • Verify in the live DOM, not in source.

6 · Voice, in one page

Flat, factual, devoid of emotion. Summaries orient the reader; they do not entertain.

  • The fact never bends for the joke.
  • The narrator is never above the reader.
  • Nothing is invented. Ever.

Personality lives in exactly two slots. The referee's one interesting fact — the only place tone is licensed on the page, and the thing you repeat to your mate at kick-off. And the sign-off: one human-written line at the end of the snapshot, sixty seconds a day, and the reason the page feels like a person made it.

Banned: exclamation marks · "only time will tell" · "fans will be delighted" · "massive blow" · "huge boost" · "masterclass" · "crucial", "vital", "must-win" · GOAT, cooked, washed, based · "it remains to be seen" · emoji in section headers.

Serious news is a different register entirely. Facts, one sentence. Human response, one sentence. No wit, no hook.
