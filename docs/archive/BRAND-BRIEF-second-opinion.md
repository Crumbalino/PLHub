# The Football Hub — brand brief for a second opinion

**Prepared 25 August 2026.** For an independent read on the brand identity. The product is settled; the identity is not.

---

## 0. What I need from you

I've been going round in circles on the visual identity and I think the reason is that I've been picking options instead of establishing a principle. I want a second opinion from someone who hasn't been in the room.

**The specific questions are in §7.** Everything before that is context, and §4 is the important part — it's what I've actually reacted to, positively and negatively, over about thirty rounds.

Please don't just validate. If the premise is wrong, say so.

---

## 1. The product

**A football fan's starting point. One page per Premier League club, plus a cross-league homepage.**

The problem is not a shortage of football information — it's that following one club means a daily circuit of five or six places. Mine, for twenty years: BBC gossip column, then NewsNow for Spurs news (clickbait, intrusive ads, the actual story buried at the bottom to game dwell time), then Reddit for the discourse, then YouTube in the evening for a creator I have to go and find.

This site collapses that circuit into one page. Everything truncated to the facts. Scannable, bounded, and it ends.

**Four questions, in order: What just happened? What matters? What's next? What's worth my time?**

Consumable in two minutes. A jumping-off point if you've got twenty.

### The rules that govern every decision

1. **We don't create football news. We organise it.** The moment a decision makes this a publisher, it's the wrong decision. No original articles, no AI-written content, no editorial team.
2. **Host the data, link out for editorial.** Facts and figures live on pages we own. Reporting, analysis and discussion link out prominently — that's what makes adding value on other people's work acceptable.
3. **The snapshot is a snapshot.** Bounded, scannable, finite. Depth is a click to a page we own, never an expansion in place.

### The things that make it different

- **Stories, not articles.** Twenty articles about the same rumour become one entry with the outlets shown and counted. NewsNow shows twenty rows; we show one thing and say how many people are saying it.
- **Counts, never verdicts.** "Six outlets" is a fact. "Widely reported" is an assertion. There is no truth score and no qualitative ladder — the reader draws their own conclusion. *This constraint matters for colour: anything that reads as approval is a problem.*
- **Ordered by movement, not recency.** A story that had two outlets this morning and six now outranks something filed eleven minutes ago that nobody else touched. Reverse-chronological reshuffles because someone hit publish, which isn't information.
- **Curated, not comprehensive.** We throw 90% away. The source whitelist is manual and it's the moat, because it's judgement rather than code.
- **Reddit and long-form creators surfaced per club.** Nobody else does either.

### Monetisation

A daily email — "The Snapshot" — is the mechanism, not a bolt-on. It's what makes the site a habit rather than a bookmark you forget. Target is roughly £60k/year at 24–30 months across newsletter, sponsorship, brand deals and a floor of display.

## 2. The user

Me. Forties, ADHD, two small kids, work from home, read about my club in stolen moments all day. True or fake, I'm interested either way.

**What that means for design, as consequences rather than a persona exercise:**

- Immediate orientation. Nothing to decode.
- Small chunks, predictable structure, the same blocks in the same places every day.
- Scans before reading — so the design should scan *for* you.
- Bounded. The page ends, and finishing is the reward.
- Depth available, never forced.
- Never demands attention. Ready whenever there are two spare minutes.

**This is not "an ADHD football site."** No mode, no label, no announcement. It should just feel *weirdly easy to use* — which also serves someone tired, dyslexic, on a small screen, or cooking dinner.

## 3. Where the design has got to

**Agreed direction:** it's a board, not a page. Football sites all look alike because they inherited the newspaper sports section — big photo, big headline, feed below. A departure board is a different object: typographic, dense, information-first, photography incidental.

**Which means hierarchy comes from type, not decoration.** If a page needs boxes, badges and background colours to communicate hierarchy, the typography isn't working.

**Explicitly avoiding:** grass green, stadium imagery, aggressive sports type, red breaking-news banners, betting aesthetics, generic score-app design. Also the Linear/Raycast dark-developer-tool look — distinctive within football, completely generic outside it.

**Reference point that fits:** Strava. Sport that doesn't look like sports media.

**Competitors to be visually distinct from:** FotMob, OneFootball, Sky Sports, BBC Sport, NewsNow, Fantasy Premier League.

## 4. The reaction record

This is the useful part. What I've responded to over thirty-odd rounds, in my own words where possible.

### Liked

| Thing | What I said |
|---|---|
| **The Spurs THFC monogram** | "Looks football and looks clean" — too busy for what I want, but owning "TFH" means I can use it in copy throughout |
| **The slash** | Came back to it repeatedly. "God I really like the slash." Liked it enough that I nearly renamed the whole product Slash Football |
| **Two-tone wordmark** | One word of the name in colour, the rest in white |
| **Corner brackets framing a word** | "I really like Hub in the brackets" — and liked the favicon that came out of it |
| **Lowercase, tightly set** | "All closed together and lower case looks nice" |
| **An iridescent gradient sphere** (from a stock "Revival" logo) | "Something about it speaks to me" — knowing it contradicts everything else I've said |
| **Minimal line art with a gradient stroke** | A reference image: thin pen-nib mark, dark background, colour as a filament, dotted trail |
| **`#F2F6FA` ink on `#05070C`** | Explicitly picked it out of a specimen. This is the one colour decision I'm confident about |
| **Premier League's bold use of colour** | Admired — but their colours are decoration on a brand nobody needs convincing about. Mine have to be earned |
| **Green** | Agreed `#22E08A` at one point, then it got dropped, and I asked for it back |

### Rejected, and why

| Thing | Why |
|---|---|
| **Grey, in any form, forever** | Hate it. Non-negotiable |
| **Khaki, beige, taupe** | Same. Appeared when a warm accent was desaturated to make a mid-tone |
| **Anything "dim"** | "Nothing dim — no colour on dim version of the same colour." A faded version of a colour is not a colour |
| **Pastel `#9BE8FF` + `#FF8FC4`** | "Gender reveal powder pink and blue." These were arrived at by desaturating to hit contrast targets, which is how they ended up in a nursery |
| **Three dots as a device** | Didn't like it |
| **A bracket forming the H of "Hub"** | "Disgusting — the football fub?" Legibility failure |
| **A separate symbol plus a wordmark** | Two things is one too many. Whatever the mark is, it should be one object |
| **A camera/framing device** | On the nose, given "the snapshot" is the newsletter name |
| **Green-to-cyan gradients** | Fantasy Premier League's identity is exactly that, and we consume the FPL API, so it'd read as affiliation |
| **"Slash Football" as a name** | Investigated, and an active UK football brand already owns it — 3.3M Facebook, 46K Instagram, YouTube channel. Dead end |

### The pattern I can see in that list

Everything I've liked is **structural** — a separator, a frame, a letter carrying colour. Everything I've rejected is **applied** — a symbol placed next to the name, or a colour softened to fit a gap.

And everything muddy came from the same move: **lowering the intensity of a colour to make something recede.** That's what produced the grey, the khaki and the pastels. All three are the same mistake wearing different clothes.

## 5. Decided vs assumed

Please treat these differently. A lot of what looks settled is one person's recommendation that I never explicitly agreed to.

### Decided by me

- **Name:** The Football Hub. Domain `thefootballhub.uk`, owned, DNS wired, zero indexed pages
- **Ground:** `#05070C` — near-black, not pure black
- **Ink:** `#F2F6FA` — cool white
- **White on black, high contrast, bold, large text**
- **No grey. No khaki. Nothing dim.**
- **The logo is a wordmark**, not a badge, crest or symbol
- **Colour must be earned** and must have a reason to exist. One or two flourish colours, used sparingly
- **The slash is a device I want to keep** in some form

### Recommended to me, not confirmed

- Body text at 17px rather than the sports-site default of 15
- Atkinson Hyperlegible as the text face (commissioned by the Braille Institute, engineered so no two characters can be confused) with Archivo for the wordmark and IBM Plex Mono for labels. **I never responded to this specimen — the typeface is genuinely undecided**
- Square corners at 2px rather than rounded, on the grounds that rounded is what every score app already looks like
- Amber `#F0A32A` as the single accent, on the grounds that warm-on-cool is the only unclaimed temperature in the category
- Green `#22E08A` reserved for `CONFIRMED` only, on the grounds that green means "true" and that's the one place it would be telling the truth
- A rule that gradients never touch letterforms, because gradient type has no single measurable contrast value

### Hard technical constraints

- Every colour needs a measured WCAG contrast ratio against the ground. Nothing enters the palette without one
- Pure white on pure black (21:1) is **not** the target — halation makes small text bleed at the edges, worst for exactly this reader
- 11px minimum font size, 44×44px minimum touch target, 320px minimum viewport
- The mark must survive a 16px favicon
- Status must always be a word, never colour alone
- Design tokens as plain TypeScript objects, never CSS custom properties — undefined CSS variables render components invisibly *and pass every build check*, which has broken this site twice
- Fonts self-hosted, no Google Fonts link

## 6. The unresolved tension

I want to state this plainly because I think it's the actual problem, not a detail.

**I've banned grey, khaki, and dim tones. I've also said colour must be earned and used sparingly. Those two rules together mean there is nothing between full-strength ink and a full-strength accent.**

A dense information page normally builds hierarchy with a mid-tier — the thing that lets a timestamp sit quietly under a headline. I've removed the entire tool.

So either:

- **Hierarchy comes only from size, weight, typeface and letterspacing** — which is the board idea taken literally, and a departure board genuinely doesn't dim anything. Risk: nothing recedes, and a dense page reads as loud.
- **Or there's a mid-tier that is unambiguously a colour** rather than a faded neutral — a real blue, say, doing a quiet job. Risk: it's a third colour to keep in line, and it starts to look like a palette rather than a system.
- **Or hierarchy comes from surface and structure** — background panels, rules, spacing — which I've mostly ruled out as decoration.

I don't know which is right and I'd like a view.

---

## 7. What I'm asking

1. **Is "no grey, nothing dim" workable on a dense information page, or am I removing a tool I'll need?** If it's workable, what carries hierarchy instead?

2. **Given the reaction record in §4 — structural devices in, applied symbols out — what should the mark actually be?** The slash keeps coming back. Is that a real signal or am I just attached to it?

3. **What single accent colour survives all of this?** It can't be Premier League purple/magenta/cyan/green, can't be FPL green-to-cyan, can't be pastel, can't be grey or khaki, must clear roughly 7:1 on `#05070C`, and must not imply that a story is *true* — because the product's central promise is that it won't tell you that.

4. **Is warm-on-cool actually the differentiator it's been claimed to be**, or is that a neat argument that won't survive contact with a real page?

5. **The typeface is open.** Given ADHD, scanning, small screens and a lot of numbers and abbreviations — what would you choose, and why not the obvious ones?

6. **What am I not seeing?** Thirty rounds in, I'd rather hear that the premise is wrong than get another set of options.

## Appendix — what the page actually contains

So the identity is judged against real content, not a moodboard. Per club:

**Match block** (phase-aware — live / last match / next match / off-week), **availability** (injuries, suspensions, return dates), **the referee** (name, cards per game, record against this club, one interesting hand-written fact), **key data** (top scorer, most assists, in form, one booking away from suspension), **table** (three above, you, three below), **form** (last five), **next opponent** (their form, their big absence, their biggest story), **the numbers** (position, points, goal difference, xG), **confirmed** (official announcements, one line each), **the big story** and **developing** (consolidated, with outlet counts), **around the league**, **worth your time** (curated long-form video and journalism), **fan pulse** (Reddit threads, titles and scores only), and a **sign-off** — one human-written line, the completion signal.

Seventeen blocks. Thirteen can ship before any clustering exists.
