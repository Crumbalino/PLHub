01
Brand Philosophy
Why The Football Hub exists, how it works, and what it is. The foundation that everything else in this document builds on.
Why
Football content is broken for the people who've been watching the longest.
If you're 35 and you've been following the Premier League for two decades, the internet has decided you don't exist. Everything is built for the scroll — clickbait headlines, breathless BREAKING alerts that turn out to be nothing, gambling odds baked into every page, and a tone of voice that sounds like it was written by a group chat of teenagers.
The people who actually know football — who've seen the cycles, who can spot a manufactured headline from across the room — have been left to fend for themselves across six different apps every morning.
The Football Hub exists because football fans deserve a place that respects their time and their intelligence equally. Not a community to join. Not a platform to perform on. Just a place that tells you what happened, whether you should care, and lets you get on with your day.
How

We frame it. You're caught up.

That's the Snapshot — The Football Hub's core idea. Every story, every score, every take is framed for you. AI reads the sources so you don't have to. The summary gives you the picture in two sentences. The Take gives you The Football Hub's editorial take in one more tap. The Hub Index tells you what's actually worth your attention, scored by the community, not by an editor chasing clicks.
One editorial voice runs through everything — the site, social media, the newsletter, The Take. It sounds like the smartest person at your table in the pub. Informed, casual, human. Opinions held lightly. Dry wit over loud takes. Not a character. Not a persona. Just a consistent voice that respects your intelligence and your time equally.
The how is also about what we refuse to do. No gambling content — ever. No clickbait. No internet slang that signals belonging to a tribe you're not in. No autoplay. No infinite scroll traps. No content that makes you feel worse for having read it.
The design is ADHD-friendly first. High contrast. Large, scannable fonts. Bite-sized AI summaries. Rewarding interactions. Get what you need and move on. The site should feel like a nice place to be, not an anxiety machine.
What
The Football Hub is a football news aggregation platform. Web-first, mobile-optimised, eventually an app. Starting with the Premier League and expanding to cover all football.
The core product is The Snapshot — a daily briefing that gives you the full picture of what's happening in football right now. Live table, today's fixtures, the stories that matter, one AI-generated summary per story. One look and you're caught up.
Every story lives in a StoryCard — source-labelled, AI-summarised, community-scored by the Hub Index, with an expandable editorial opinion called The Take. Content is aggregated from BBC, Sky, Guardian, talkSPORT, Goal.com, 90min, Reddit, and YouTube — then scored, summarised, and served without the noise.
Future: a React Native app with The Snapshot as the home tab. A daily newsletter. Match-day social content that uses the Snapshot frame as a recognisable visual signature across every platform.
The bracket pair — the diagonal frame — is the visual embodiment of everything The Football Hub does. We frame the story. You're caught up. That's it.

03
Logo System
One teal bracket. All white text. The bracket is the brand — everything else follows from it. Pink bracket is the variant: social, off-site, and high-energy moments. Never both on the same surface.
The Design Decision
The bracket is top-left only on the wordmark. The full diagonal frame (TL + BR pair) belongs exclusively to The Snapshot as a content device. This distinction is structural — it means the logo can never be confused with a framed piece of content, and the Snapshot frame always reads as a curation signal rather than branding noise.
Teal is the primary bracket colour. Teal already carries structure, data, and reliability across the product — scores, table positions, interactive states. The logo bracket being teal means the mark reads as part of the same system, not as a separate injection of energy. It's coherent. The pink variant is reserved for moments that need friction and attention: social posts at scroll speed, off-site appearances, and breaking news.
All white text. The weight contrast between Sora 700 on "FOOTBALL" and JetBrains Mono 300 on "HUB" does all the differentiation work. Adding teal to HUB splits the accent across two points. One bracket hit is stronger than two. On light surfaces, the wordmark flips to navy — the bracket always remains its assigned colour.
Three-word hierarchy — one rule. "the" is the least important word. "FOOTBALL" is the claim. "HUB" is the category. The mark encodes this in weight, size and opacity: "the" whispers (Sora 200, lowercase, 45% opacity), "FOOTBALL" shouts (Sora 700, 100%), "HUB" labels (JetBrains Mono 300, 85%). The domain availability of thefootballhub.uk isn't a compromise — "the" is a quiet authority claim. Not a football hub. The football hub. That's the concept.
"the" disappears gracefully. At XS and nav sizes, "the" drops below visible threshold. The mark reads cleanly as FOOTBALL HUB. Nothing breaks — the least important element exits first, leaving the mark stronger.

The No Grey Rule
No grey hex codes. Anywhere. Ever. Text hierarchy is built entirely with opacity on the base colour. This keeps everything connected to the palette instead of introducing dead neutral tones.
Readability is the USP. Hierarchy comes from size and weight first, colour second, opacity last. The minimum opacity floor is 40% — anything below that fails the ADHD-friendly promise.

Card Types
The feed is made of cards. Two families: RSS cards (NewsCard — auto-pulled from sources) and Content cards (curated from our own Supabase tables). The card demos on this page are the single source of truth — taken directly from the approved tfh-feed-v5.jsx prototype. Do not deviate from these designs.
Single source of truth. These are the approved, implemented card designs from tfh-feed-v5.jsx. All implementation must match these exactly. No top-zone coloured blocks. No 4px borders. No assumptions.
Family A — NewsCard (RSS)
All RSS stories render as a NewsCard. One template. One implementation. The left border colour signals the publisher. The card does not change shape or structure based on whether it is a stat, result, or quote — the headline carries that information. Detection subtypes (Stat, Result, Quote, LOL, Rumour) exist in the system for scoring and filtering only — they do not change the visual template.
Border = 3px solid publisher colour. Not 4px. No top-zone coloured blocks. No separate stat/quote/result card templates. There is one NewsCard. The publisher colour left border is how you know the source.

Card Interaction System
Every card type has defined interactive behaviour. This section is the complete specification. If a card type and interaction state isn't covered here, it doesn't exist.
The Core Principle
There are two layers to every non-news, non-fixture card:
Layer 1 — Primary interaction: The card's core mechanic. This is what reveals the content. Every card type has a different trigger and reveal animation.
Layer 2 — Your Verdict: Unlocks only after Layer 1 completes. The user's emotional response to what they just read. It is a reward for engaging, not a cold prompt. You cannot get to Your Verdict without completing the primary interaction first.
Why this order matters: If Your Verdict appeared on a cold card (before the user had read anything), it would be meaningless — "what's your verdict on something you haven't read yet?" By making it a reward for engaging, the reaction means something when it appears. It says: "You've just seen something. What did you think?"

Your Verdict
The Football Hub's reaction system. Ten emoji reactions. Anonymous. No logins. No scores. No gamification beyond the feedback itself. It makes the feed feel alive.
What It Is
Your Verdict lets users express how they feel about a piece of content with a single tap. It is not a rating system (no stars, no scores). It is not social (no usernames, no public activity feeds). It is not gamified beyond the progress bar. The reactions exist to give the user a moment of expression and to surface aggregate sentiment. They make the feed feel alive. That is the entire brief.

Data Model
Server side (Supabase): Aggregate counts only. No user data stored. Table: card_reactions with fields: card_id, card_type, reaction_idx (0–9), count, updated_at.
Client side (session only): sessionStorage key tfh_voted_${cardId} prevents double-voting on refresh. Does not persist across sessions — anonymous and stateless from the user's perspective.
Seeding: All cards are seeded with realistic vote counts at launch. The reaction grid must never feel empty. Seed distribution weights reactions 0–3 (the most football-specific) higher.

Principles
ease-out for enters, ease-in for exits. Never linear — it feels mechanical.

Brand Rules
The definitive do and don't list. If you're unsure, check here first.
Do
Use pink brackets on all logo tiers — both brackets, always pink
Keep text in surface colour (white on dark, navy on light) — never pink text in the logo
Use 700/300 weight contrast for PL/HUB — this is what separates them
Use teal for structural, interactive, and data elements as defined in Section 08
Use a single opening bracket (TL) for Hub Index scores — one bracket for data, two brackets for content frames
Use the framing treatment (TL/BR corners) for The Take, Snapshot modules, and social templates
Test all elements in both dark and light mode
Use opacity on the base colour for text hierarchy — never grey hex codes
Frame content with brackets at reduced opacity for Snapshot moments
Use the framed inline logo in nav (brackets on outside, diagonal offset)
Respect prefers-reduced-motion for all animations
Maintain 44×44px minimum touch targets
Write in British English, Oxford comma, sentence case headlines
Don't
Never use gold in the logo — gold is editorial only (BBC Sport label)
Never use pink text for PL in the wordmark — that's v2, not v3
Never complete the frame with four brackets — always two, diagonal only
Never rotate or angle the diagonal frame mark
No gambling content, branding, or sponsorship — ever
Never use pure black (#000000) as a background — always navy
Never use grey hex codes for text — use opacity on the base colour
Never use warm white for light mode backgrounds — cool white only
Never autoplay video, audio, or infinite looping animations
Never use fonts other than Sora (except monospace for scores)
Never use ⚡ or the full bracket pair for scores — the single opening bracket is the Hub Index icon
Never use AI pundit characters — one editorial voice only
The Three Non-Negotiables
1. No gambling content. Ever. Hardcoded. Don't ask.
2. Respect the reader's intelligence. Never explain what doesn't need explaining. Never shout what can be said. Our audience has been watching football longer than most content creators have been alive.
3. Pub, not internet. If it wouldn't be said in a real conversation between real adults, it doesn't belong on The Football Hub.
